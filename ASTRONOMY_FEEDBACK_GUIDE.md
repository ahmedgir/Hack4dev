# Astronomy Friend Feedback Guide

Use this guide with the two astronomy enthusiasts after the first raw image and light-curve draft are visible. A 15-20 minute session is enough.

## Explain the project in one sentence

We are building an Arabic/English interactive tool that shows a non-astronomer how raw telescope images become evidence of a known exoplanet transit, including why some frames are rejected and how confident the result is.

## Show them

1. One raw FITS image rendered with a useful intensity stretch.
2. The target and proposed comparison stars.
3. The first raw light curve.
4. The curve before and after quality filtering.
5. The proposed plain-language explanation.

## Ask these questions

1. What did you think the image showed before reading our explanation?
2. Can you identify the target star after our explanation?
3. Does the transit explanation feel scientifically honest?
4. Which displayed measurement increases your confidence most?
5. Which measurement or warning is missing?
6. Does any wording accidentally imply that we discovered or confirmed a planet?
7. Are the rejected-frame reasons understandable and scientifically plausible?
8. Would you prefer a guided story or a free exploration interface first?
9. What would make you share this tool with another astronomy enthusiast?
10. Which technical details should remain hidden until the user asks for evidence?

## Record feedback as evidence

For each person, record:

- astronomy experience level;
- three observations;
- one confusing point;
- one requested feature;
- one wording correction;
- whether they understood the core story without help.

Do not ask them to design the product. Observe what they understand, where they hesitate, and which evidence they trust.
